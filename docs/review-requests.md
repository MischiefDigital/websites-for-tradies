# Review requests — templates and timing

Audit section 06. Reviews are the biggest lever in local search and the one
most businesses never pull properly.

**The founding deal already trades NZ$300 for an honest review.** That's the
hard part done — the ask is pre-agreed. What follows is about making it easy to
say yes and getting the review onto Google rather than into a text message.

---

## The three rules

1. **Ask within 48 hours of launch day.** Excitement decays fast. A tradie who
   just watched their site go live will write you a paragraph. The same tradie
   three weeks later writes "good service, cheers".
2. **Send a direct link.** "Could you leave us a Google review?" converts badly
   because the person has to go find you. A tappable link converts several times
   better.
3. **Google first, always.** Google reviews affect local ranking. Trustpilot and
   Facebook don't. Only ask for a second review somewhere else after the Google
   one is up.

---

## Getting your direct review link

1. Open your Google Business Profile
2. **Ask for reviews** → copy the short link (`g.page/r/...`)
3. Paste it into the templates below where they say `[REVIEW LINK]`

Save it somewhere you can find it in five seconds. If it takes effort to
retrieve, you'll skip the ask.

---

## Template 1 — the launch-day text

Send within 48 hours. Text, not email — tradies read texts.

```
Hey [NAME], site's live and looking sharp: [THEIR WEBSITE URL]

You mentioned you'd do an honest review once it was up — here's the link, takes about a minute: [REVIEW LINK]

Genuinely want the honest version, not a favour. If something about the process annoyed you, say that too — it's more useful to me than five stars.

Cheers,
Blair
```

**Why it's worded that way:** asking for honesty rather than praise gets better
reviews, not worse ones. It also matches the "no fake reviews" positioning the
whole website runs on — asking for a glowing review would contradict the page
that says you'd never invent one.

---

## Template 2 — the follow-up

If nothing after five days. **Once only.** Two chases and you're a nuisance.

```
Hey [NAME], no stress if you've been flat out — just bumping this in case it got buried.

[REVIEW LINK]

If you'd rather not, that's genuinely fine, just say and I'll stop asking.
```

That last line matters. It gives them an easy out, which makes the ask feel
less like pressure — and people who are given an out more often do the thing.

---

## Template 3 — the 90-day check-in

Sent at the end of the guarantee period. Does three jobs at once: confirms the
guarantee was met, gets a results-based review, and opens the door to a case
study.

```
Hey [NAME], we're at the 90-day mark since your site went live.

Two quick things:

1. Has the site brought you any job enquiries? Even one counts — that's the guarantee. If the answer's no, tell me and we start the rebuild, no argument.

2. If it has, and you're happy to say so, an updated review mentioning the actual result is worth more than anything I could write myself. [REVIEW LINK]

Either way I'd like to know how it's going.

Blair
```

---

## Template 4 — asking for a case study

Only after a review is up and the results are real.

```
Hey [NAME], that review's much appreciated.

Bigger ask: would you be up for me writing your build up as a case study? Rough numbers on enquiries before and after, a couple of photos of your work, and a line or two from you.

It'd go on the WFT site and I'd send you the draft before anything goes live — nothing gets published you haven't seen.

Completely fine to say no.
```

**Why ask separately:** case studies with real numbers are what the audit calls
the proof engine, and they're worth more than reviews. But asking for both at
once dilutes the review request, which is the one that affects ranking.

---

## Replying to reviews

Reply to every one within 24 hours. Replies are public and read by people
deciding whether to call you.

**Name the trade and the town.** It reads as genuine and it puts local terms on
your profile naturally.

**Good:**
```
Cheers Dave — glad the site's already pulling enquiries in Porirua. Give us a yell when you're ready to add the bathroom renovation pages.
```

**Bad:**
```
Thank you for your 5 star review! We appreciate your business!
```

The second one is obviously templated, adds nothing, and every business in the
country writes it.

### Replying to a bad review

It will happen. The reply is not for the reviewer — it's for the next fifty
people who read it.

```
Hi [NAME], thanks for being straight about this and sorry it fell short.

[Specific acknowledgement of what actually went wrong — no excuses.]

[What you're doing about it.]

I'd like to sort it properly — I'll call you today, or you can reach me on [PHONE].
```

Never argue, never blame the client, never mention what they did wrong even if
they did. A calm, specific reply to a bad review builds more trust than a wall
of five stars.

## Targets

From the audit's measurement table:

| Milestone | Target |
|---|---|
| 90 days | 5–10 reviews from the founding cohort |
| 12 months | 30+ reviews, 4.9 average |

The audit's assessment: **ten genuine 5-star reviews with photos likely puts
WFT ahead of every NZ competitor's profile in this niche**, and neutralises the
Australian namesake's Trustpilot presence in brand searches.

Ten. That's the whole target. It's achievable from the founding cohort alone.
